# Interview Synthesis — Retention (3 users)

Source: interview excerpts from Priya S. (retained, 18 months), Tom R. (churned, left after 5 weeks), Amara L. (new, 10 days in).

## Top 5 Themes

### 1. The app goes static right after initial setup
Once the early setup is done, nothing in the experience changes day to day or week to week — for a churned user and a brand-new user alike.

> "The app just showed me the same dashboard every time." — Tom R.

> "I check it occasionally but nothing has changed since the first day." — Amara L.

### 2. Users want to be told what to do next, not just shown data
Both a churned user and a current new user describe waiting for direction that never came.

> "I kept waiting for it to give me something to act on and it never really did." — Tom R.

> "I have been waiting for Nudge to tell me the next step." — Amara L.

### 3. Specific, personally-surprising insights create the "aha" moment
The moments that landed for users weren't generic — they were sharp, individual facts about the user's own behavior.

> "The spending breakdown was shocking in a useful way — I had no idea I was spending that much on subscriptions." — Amara L.

> "The thing that made it stick for me was when I noticed it started surfacing patterns I had not consciously registered — like that I always overspend in the last week of the month." — Priya S.

### 4. Long-term engagement becomes ritual once the app demonstrates it "knows" the user
For the one retained user in this set, the payoff wasn't a feature — it was a felt sense of being understood, which turned into a weekly habit.

> "I open Nudge every Sunday morning before I start my week. It has become a ritual." — Priya S.

> "I feel like Nudge actually knows my financial habits now." — Priya S.

### 5. Time-to-value is slow relative to user patience — the payoff and the churn window don't line up
The retained user's payoff took months; the churned user was gone in weeks, and had already looked elsewhere for a more responsive experience.

> "But it took about three months to get there and I think most people give up before that." — Priya S.

> "I switched to YNAB because at least that feels like it is asking something of me." — Tom R.

## Contradictions / Tensions

- **Two different "aha" moments, unclear which one matters for retention.** Amara's aha was immediate (day 1, from the spending breakdown itself). Priya's aha — the one she credits with making the app stick — didn't arrive until ~3 months in, from a deeper behavioral pattern. It's unresolved whether the day-1 insight is supposed to be sufficient on its own, or whether it's just the first of several insights users need to reach before retention takes hold.

- **Doing the "right" onboarding steps didn't protect Tom from churning.** Tom connected three accounts and set up a budget — exactly the kind of week-1 engagement the team's data associates with lower churn (per Raj's finding that goal-setters churn less) — yet he still left after 5 weeks. This suggests taking the onboarding actions isn't enough by itself if nothing meaningful follows afterward.

- **Priya's own account of the timeline undercuts the current experience.** She explicitly believes "most people give up" before reaching the 3-month point where the app paid off for her — meaning her success story may describe an experience that mostly serves unusually patient users, not the typical new user.

## Single Most Important Insight for Week-1 Retention

The day-1 "aha" (the spending breakdown) is necessary but not sufficient — it creates a moment of interest, not a habit. Amara is already stalling at day 10 with no next action, and Tom's churn shows that even users who complete meaningful setup work still leave if nothing follows. Priya's real payoff didn't arrive for three months, which by her own estimate is longer than most users will wait. This means the critical window for week-1 retention isn't about delivering a bigger first insight — it's about giving users a concrete, personal reason to come back in the days immediately after that first insight, well before month three.
