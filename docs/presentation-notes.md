# Quarterly Review — Speaker Notes

## Slide 1 — The Problem

This is where we are: 30-day retention has dropped from 44% to 37% over the last two quarters.  
That's not a blip — it's a real decline in the share of new users who are still active a month after they connect their first account.  
The reason, based on what we've heard directly from users, is that the app gives people a strong first moment — the spending breakdown — and then nothing after that to bring them back.  
I want you to leave this slide with one thing: we have a real, measured retention problem, and we understand roughly why it's happening.

## Slide 2 — Why Now

Here's what's changed since we last talked about this: we didn't just theorize a fix, we tested one.  
We ran a real, controlled pilot — fifty users got a personalized weekly summary, fifty got nothing — and for the first time in this project, we have actual quantitative data instead of just interviews and surveys.  
The early signal is strong: users who got the summary came back at a significantly higher rate within the first week, and the notification kept getting opened week after week instead of being tuned out.  
The number that matters most long-term, 30-day retention, also moved — a lot — but not yet by an amount we can call statistically proven.  
Take away from this slide that we have real evidence the idea works in the short term, and a promising but unconfirmed signal on the number you actually care about.

## Slide 3 — The Proposal

I want to be precise about what I'm proposing, because I don't want to overstate it.  
What this is: expanding the pilot into a properly sized test — about twenty-three times the current sample — before we make any call on a full rollout.  
What this isn't: it's not a decision to ship this to everyone yet, and it's not a commitment to build the more complex debt-aware version of the nudge, which is still an unvalidated idea we've deliberately kept out of scope.  
The takeaway I want you to leave with is that this is a request to keep testing rigorously, not a request to greenlight a launch.

## Slide 4 — Evidence

This is what the experience actually looks like and what real users have told us they need.  
The screenshot shows the weekly summary itself — a specific insight, a clear one-tap action, and visible progress toward a goal the user already set.  
The quotes on this slide aren't hypothetical — they're from real interviews: a churned user told us he kept waiting for the app to give him something to act on and it never did, and a newer user said almost the same thing in her own words.  
The data backs up that this design addresses it: the day-seven lift and the notification open rate are both statistically significant, not just directionally nice.  
What I want you to take away here is that this isn't a guess — it's grounded in what users told us and in what we've already measured.

## Slide 5 — The Plan

Here's the actual plan if you approve this.  
The test needs to run for roughly five to seven weeks to reach a sample size we can trust, and that comfortably fits inside an eight-week window — though I still need to confirm our real weekly enrollment pace with the data team before I commit to an exact date.  
We won't be flying blind for two months: we're tracking three leading indicators every week — day-seven retention, the summary's open rate, and one specific behavioral pattern we want to watch — so we'll know early if something's going wrong rather than finding out at the very end.  
The honest risk on both sides: if we wait, retention keeps eroding at the rate we've already documented; if we scale before we're sure, we risk spending real engineering time on a result that has a real chance of not holding up.  
I want you to leave this slide trusting that we have a plan to know the answer quickly, not just a request for more time.

## Slide 6 — The Ask

This is what I actually need from you today.  
First, sign-off to extend the experiment to the properly powered sample before we talk about a full rollout.  
Second, I want your agreement on a change to how we measure success — we're making day-seven retention the primary metric instead of how many people act on the nudge, because our own data shows the people who act are actually retaining worse than the people who just open it, which means the old metric could point us in the wrong direction.  
Third, I don't have an exact rollout date for you yet — I need one number from the data team first — but getting your sign-off today is what lets me come back with a real date instead of a guess.  
What I want you to walk away with is a clear yes on two decisions and a clear next step on the third.

## Slide 7 — One-Slide Fallback

If this is the only slide that survives: retention dropped from 44% to 37%, a real pilot test showed a statistically significant early improvement and a promising-but-unproven longer-term one, and the ask is approval to properly test it — about five to seven weeks — before committing to a full rollout.  
That's the whole story in one slide, and it's the version I'd want you to remember even if you forget everything else.
